[1] PIPELINE - the order matters

    raw tweet
      01 strip the [Entity] tag Doccano adds
      02 unescape HTML entities        (&amp;  -> &)
      03 remove URLs
      04 remove @mentions
      05 unwrap #hashtags              (#BoT   -> BoT)
      06 lowercase
      07 EXPAND CONTRACTIONS           (don't  -> do not)      <-- required
      08 squash elongations            (soooo  -> soo)
      09 remove emoji / digits / punctuation
      10 normalise whitespace
      11 WORD TOKENIZATION             (nltk TweetTokenizer)   <-- required
      12 STOPWORDS REMOVAL             (nltk, negations kept)  <-- required
      13 LEMMATIZATION                 (WordNet + POS tags)    <-- required
      14 drop 1-character tokens

    Two ordering decisions worth defending in the write-up:
      * contraction expansion (07) MUST come before punctuation removal (09).
        Reverse them and "don't" becomes "dont", which no expander recognises.
      * lemmatization (13) comes after stopword removal (12) so we lemmatize
        fewer tokens, and after lowercasing so WordNet lookups actually hit.

[3] WHY LEMMATIZATION AND NOT STEMMING
    The brief says choose one. I chose lemmatization because Porter stemming
    mangles words into non-words, which makes the cleaned corpus unreadable
    for the sentiment analysis this dataset is for:

    word             PorterStemmer      WordNetLemmatizer 
    murdering        murder             murder            
    coming           come               come              
    games            game               game              
    better           better             good              
    crashes          crash              crash             
    really           realli             really            
    terrible         terribl            terrible          
    communities      commun             community         
    was              wa                 be                
    studying         studi              study             

    'terrible' -> 'terribl' and 'really' -> 'realli' are the clearest losses:
    both are strong sentiment carriers and both survive lemmatization intact.


[4] STOPWORDS - what I removed and what I deliberately kept
    nltk english stopword list : 198 words
    words I put back           : 31
    effective stopword list    : 172 words

    Kept on purpose: against, aren, but, cannot, couldn, didn, doesn, don, down, hadn, hasn, haven, isn, n't, never, no, none, nor, not, nothing, off, over, shouldn, too, under, up, very, wasn, weren, won, wouldn
    Reason: negation flips polarity. Dropping 'not' turns "not good" into
    "good", which would silently corrupt every Negative tweet in the corpus.
